Thermal Cycling Reliability of Copper Wire Bonds in Power Modules

Liang Zhang and Kenji Tanaka
Center for Electronic Packaging and Integration, Institute of Materials Science
Abstract—Copper wire bonding has emerged as a cost-effective replacement for gold in power modules, yet its long-term reliability under cyclic thermal stress remains a central concern. In this work, 25 µm palladium-coated and bare copper wires were bonded onto aluminum metallized substrates and subjected to thermal cycling between −40 °C and +140 °C for up to 2,000 cycles. The shear strength of copper wire bonds degrades progressively with thermal cycling, driven primarily by interfacial intermetallic growth and crack coalescence at the bond interface. Palladium coating delayed the onset of degradation by approximately 400 cycles. A Paris-law crack growth model, calibrated against the measured strength retention data, predicts bond lifetimes within 12% of experimental observations.

1. Introduction

Copper offers superior electrical and thermal conductivity compared with gold, at a substantially lower material cost. Its adoption in fine-pitch power modules, however, is limited by oxidation during bonding and by the formation of brittle interfacial intermetallic compounds (IMCs) during operation.

Prior studies have characterized IMC growth kinetics under isothermal aging, but few have connected growth kinetics to the evolution of mechanical strength under realistic thermal cycling profiles. This paper addresses that gap through a combined experimental and modeling study.

2. Experimental Procedure

Bare Cu and Pd-coated Cu wires (25 µm diameter) were thermosonically bonded onto 3 µm Al-metallized Si substrates. Bonding parameters were optimized by design-of-experiments to achieve a consistent as-bonded shear strength of 78 ± 3 MPa. Specimens were subjected to air-to-air thermal cycling between −40 °C and +140 °C, with a 15-minute dwell at each extreme.

Fig. 1. Average bond shear strength versus number of thermal cycles (ΔT = 180 K) for palladium-coated and bare copper wires.

Shear tests were performed on 20 bonds per condition per readout point, and fracture surfaces were examined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) to identify failure modes.

3. Results and Discussion

After 1,000 cycles, the average shear strength retention of the palladium-coated copper wires remained above 82%, whereas bare copper wires retained only 61%. Strength loss accelerated beyond 1,500 cycles for bare copper, coinciding with the coalescence of interfacial voids into a continuous crack network.

SEM fractography revealed two distinct failure modes: ductile bulk-wire fracture dominated below 500 cycles, while brittle interfacial fracture along the IMC layer dominated thereafter. The Cu–Al IMC layer grew with a t^0.5 dependence, reaching 2.4 µm after 2,000 cycles in bare copper bonds.

The exponent m ≈ 3.2 obtained here is consistent with values reported for thick-film metallization systems, suggesting a shared crack-growth mechanism.

5. Conclusions

Surface finish and atmosphere control during bonding are therefore decisive for achieving reliable copper bonds in production. Palladium coating extends usable bond lifetime by roughly 30% under the tested profile, and the calibrated Paris-law model provides a practical basis for design-for-reliability in power modules.
